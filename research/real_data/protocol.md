# Field experiment protocol

Recorded before computing held-out localization or detection outputs, 2026-10-05.

## Source and inference target

Nine supplied outdoor recordings of a DJI Air 3S and background sound, captured with a 208-channel CRY8501 / SonoCam Pi planar array on 2026-05-25. All channels have 48 kHz, 16-bit mono PCM. The polar geometry has maximum radius 0.1499944 m. Source files remain immutable; results/field/source_manifest.json stores byte hashes. These are real recordings, not simulated sound fields. Acquisition metadata and channel lengths agree, but independent clock-skew and microphone-phase calibration are unavailable.

The six static reference directions are transcribed from images 2–4 of the acquisition description. They are nominal directions, not synchronized framewise tracking truth. Evaluation measures consistency with these references, subject to hovering motion and unknown reference uncertainty. The convention is clockwise from the device reference mark, with elevation above the array plane. The supplied interface's directional quadrants identify zero as negative native y and 90 degrees as negative native x. We use physical unit vectors (-cos(el) sin(az), -cos(el) cos(az), sin(el)); the demo's pixel/FOV angle approximation is not used for physical beam steering. The whole upper hemisphere is searched; reference labels do not enter inference.

## Development and evaluation separation

Point 1 (nominal range 100 m) is the development group. Both positive takes and background may be used for algorithm development. Points 2 and 3 (150 and 200 m) are held out from algorithm/parameter selection. All six nominal reference labels are available for defining the evaluation only. No positive audio from held-out groups is inspected algorithmically before freezing the selected method. Descriptive results at point 1 will be labeled development, never pooled into the primary generalization result.

For each point, the first 5 s of its background recording estimates the noise reference. The next 5 s is reserved for detection threshold calibration. The interval [10,30) s is the negative evaluation material. Only the first 30 s of each positive recording is evaluated. Remaining recording tails (0.3–0.5 s) are uniformly excluded. Noise reference and calibration at a held-out location are an explicit deployment assumption; this is site-calibrated evaluation, not calibration-free external validation. Thresholds use only designated background calibration, separately for each method. With 25 nonoverlapping 0.2 s calibration blocks and nominal alpha=0.05, the strict empirical upper-rank rule has rank ceil((25+1)*0.95)=25; detection requires score greater than the maximum calibration score. Exchangeability is not guaranteed for outdoor temporal blocks, so this is not a formal per-site FPR guarantee.

## Processing and baselines

All methods use identical channels, sample preprocessing, pair selection, search domain and coarse-to-fine search. Start with per-channel centering, anti-aliased resampling to 16 kHz, 512-sample Hann STFT with 256-sample hop inside each 0.2 s block. Default analysis band 300–7500 Hz. Pair geometry is selected without using sound or labels. The candidates are conventional cross-spectral SRP, SRP-PHAT, coherence-weighted PHAT, an excess-power reliability mask, and a reference-assisted coherence weighting method with spectral balancing. Method details and selected constants must be frozen in results/field/frozen_config.json before held-out execution. Development variants are retained transparently.

## Outcomes and interpretation

Primary localization outcome: paired spherical angular discrepancy on every held-out positive block, regardless of detection. Also report median, p95, within 5 and 10 degrees, and separate per-recording/per-location results. Azimuth discrepancy is secondary, particularly at point 1 elevation 88.3 degrees. Primary detection outcome: TPR and FPR on the designated positive and negative blocks, with per-recording detail; positive and negative block counts must be explicit. Joint success requires detection and angular discrepancy within 10 degrees. No continuous range estimate or trajectory-tracking claim.

Temporal blocks are correlated. There are only four held-out positive recordings and two held-out negative recordings, from one aircraft and one capture day. Do not present a blockwise bootstrap as independent-sample population inference. Descriptive paired differences per recording and all recordings will be reported; any recording-level bootstrap must disclose the tiny number of independent units and must not support broad generalization. No withholding failed blocks or selecting only detections for localization.

Compute timing excludes disk access and separates preprocessing, estimation, and the 200 ms acquisition window. Do not label workstation compute time embedded-device latency.

Synthetic studies from the preceding package remain frozen historical evidence. They will not be relabeled as measurements from this array or pooled with the field recordings. The current manuscript's primary evidence will come from the field protocol.

## Development amendment before held-out access

The initial point-1 pilot found that direct background subtraction and excess-power masks suppressed the aircraft as well as the evolving background (reference variant recall 8/60; PHAT 56/60 but 58/100 false alarms). These variants are retained as unsuccessful development evidence. A supervised, direction-conditioned coherence ranking model is therefore developed using point 1 only. It uses 24 fixed, equally spaced frequency bands over 300–7500 Hz; per-candidate features contain directional coherence, not direction coordinates, absolute power, file metadata or reference labels. Positive training examples include beams steered at the nominal training reference; hard negatives are distant audio-generated PHAT candidates and background candidates. Inference generates four candidates from PHAT alone, refines them and scores their spectra. No true direction is inserted at inference. Two grouped development folds hold out one complete aircraft recording and a separate contiguous half of the sole development background recording; the latter is not an independent recording. Ridge strength is selected from {0.1,1,10,100} by balanced candidate classification loss, which is a training diagnostic rather than a localization performance claim. The final model is fitted on all permitted point-1 examples. All held-out positive audio remains unprocessed at this amendment.

## Final frozen comparison

The selected model is direction-conditioned coherence ranking (DCCR), with ridge strength 100. The ablation omits incorrect directions from aircraft recordings and selects its own ridge strength 10 using the same grouped rule. Both retain 24 features. The main comparisons are SRP-PHAT, SCOT-SRP, coherence-weighted PHAT, the unsuccessful reference-subtraction variant, DCCR, and the no-hard-negative DCCR ablation. Retaining the subtraction variant exposes the rejected physical approach. The baseline SRP result remains in the development log, but is not an additional held-out method. The no-hard-negative model shares exactly the DCCR candidate set and features at inference. Paired joint outcomes will additionally apply the full DCCR gate to each localization method, so source association is not confounded with a different detector.

The final inference uses four PHAT proposals separated by at least 12 degrees on a 1257-point upper-hemisphere direction-cosine grid. All 208 microphones enter 1124 selected pairs; offsets are 17,35,53,71,89,103 modulo 208 and pairs shorter than 0.06 m are excluded. Each candidate is refined in native horizontal direction cosines and then in angles. Five deterministic phase tests, three original-audio feature recomputations, the ridge equation, and common amplitude gains 0.001/17/1000 passed before freezing. Training candidate loss and the repeated point-1 end-to-end pilot are development evidence only. No held-out result is used to choose the constants, model or primary method.
