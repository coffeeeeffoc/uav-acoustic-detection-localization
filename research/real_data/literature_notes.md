# Literature positioning for the field study

Checked 2026-10-05. Existing original papers and bibliographic evidence remain in references/papers, references/extracted, and references/references.json. No external dataset is pooled with the supplied recordings.

1. Knapp and Carter (1976), https://doi.org/10.1109/TASSP.1976.1162830: generalized correlation and spectral weighting establish the foundation. Neither PHAT nor coherence normalization is a new contribution here.
2. DiBiase, Silverman and Brandstein (2001), https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-04619-7_8: SRP as a robust spatial search. Sparse pair use and coarse-to-fine numerical search are implementation choices, not claimed inventions.
3. Grinstein et al. (2024), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13636-024-00377-z: the publisher page and local original article cover SRP robustness, computational reductions, multiple sources, and learned SRP extensions. The field contribution must be stated as the explicit direction-conditioned band-coherence ranker and its small-data implementation, not as the first learned spatial map or first weighted SRP.
4. Valin, Michaud and Rouat (2007), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.robot.2006.08.004: the local original article averages cross-power spectra and applies SNR-derived reliability masks before localization. The simple excess-power and background-subtraction pilot is not a full reproduction of its decision-directed noise/reverberation estimator or particle tracker.
5. Donohue, Hannemann and Dietz (2007), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2007.01.013: partial phase-transform weighting is an existing alternative. Avoid claiming that phase-only processing is always the strongest classical baseline.
6. Brümann and Doclo (2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01776 and https://doi.org/10.23919/EUSIPCO63174.2024.10715232: coherent noise can degrade compact-array SRP-PHAT. Their auxiliary-microphone construction is different from this study's learned directional spectrum; no auxiliary microphone is used here.
7. Uluskan (2024), https://doi.org/10.1177/1475472X241259082: harmonic-neighborhood SRP for drone sound is prior art. The proposed 24 uniform bands do not estimate blade-passing frequency and must not be described as a newly invented harmonic detector.
8. Yang et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.23919/EUSIPCO63237.2025.11226465: local original EUSIPCO paper concerns neural drone localization with synthesized/real audio. It motivates discussing domain transfer and array-dependent learned representations. The current 208-channel field study does not reproduce that neural model.
9. Safavi et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.1109/MLSP62443.2025.11204285, and Jekateryńczuk et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.3390/app15105378: examples of documented drone detection/localization resources. This field package's six nominal positive directions are weaker than a synchronized trajectory reference and should be described accordingly.

The methodological distinction is candidate-conditioned frequency evidence. An audio-only PHAT map proposes four bearings. A small supervised ridge model evaluates a vector of normalized, direction-steered cross-spectral responses over 24 frequency bands. Training includes incorrect candidate directions from the same positive recording, addressing source association rather than only assigning one detection score to an entire window. Absolute direction, range, amplitude, filenames and held-out reference labels are excluded from model features. A no-hard-negative model and a shared detector gate isolate this association mechanism.

Do not claim unrestricted aircraft identification, multiple-aircraft separation, tracking, embedded real-time operation, a calibrated probability output, or broad statistical generalization from one aircraft/one day. The manuscript can investigate an algorithmic improvement on the documented field material while giving these limits precisely.
